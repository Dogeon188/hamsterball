# generator.py

## Purpose

The procedural generator of spec §8: `ProceduralGenerator` builds a solvable hfield `Level` for any of the five tasks at any difficulty in [0, 1], and the same (task, seed, difficulty, version) gives the same level id on every platform.

## Interface

- `LevelGenerator`: a `runtime_checkable` protocol with `tasks`, `version`, and `__call__(task, seed, difficulty) -> Level`. The env's `levels=` takes any object that satisfies it (see [env.md](../env.md)).
- `ProceduralGenerator(**overrides)`: `name = "procedural"`, `version = 4`, `tasks` = all five. Each override is a row of `TABLE` given as `(value at d = 0, value at d = 1)`, such as `ProceduralGenerator(climb_grade=(5, 10))`; an unknown name raises `TypeError`.
- `gen(task, seed, difficulty)`: the `Level`, whose `generator` block is `{name, version, task, seed, difficulty}`. `gen.generate(...)` returns `(level, attempts)`, which the golden test uses for the resample failure rate.
- `gen.params(difficulty)`: every table row at that difficulty.
- `GenerationError`: raised when 32 attempts all fail.

## How it works

- `params`: each row is linear in d between its endpoints. `cliff_height` is 0 up to d = 0.5 and linear from 0 to its d = 1 value above. Marker counts round half up (`floor(v + 0.5)`). Grades are degrees; `hazards_per_m` is spec §8's "1 per 8 m" as 1/8. Survive's movers: `survive_drift_speed` (0 → 1.5 m/s, the region's peak speed), `moving_hazards` (0 → 3, a count), `hazard_speed` (1 → 2 m/s, each moving hazard's peak speed). Survive's body layouts (ticket survive-movers/08): `obstacles` (1 → 3 pillars, a count), `obstacle_speed` (0.5 → 2 m/s peak), `platform_speed` (0.25 → 1 m/s peak over a 3 m sweep), `platform_tilt` (0 → 12°), `seesaw_stiffness` (400 → 50 N·m/rad), `seesaw_offset` (0.25 → 1.5 m, spawn to pivot), `tile_delay` (2 → 0.5 s). `survive_disc_radius` ends at 2 m (was 1.5 m, see Decisions).
- One PCG64 stream per call, seeded with `seed`; a failed attempt (`_Retry`) moves on to the stream's next draws, up to 32 attempts.
- Route tasks (`_route`), spec §8 steps 1–5:
  1. Spine: one target per leg (reach goal 1, waypoint race `waypoints`, collect items `items`, unlock goal `keys` + 1 with the goal last), each leg `route_length / n`, split into straights of at most `STRAIGHT` = 8 m. A random start heading, then a turn at each vertex up to `MAX_TURN` = 45°, smaller when straights are short (below). Vertices round to the millimetre.
  2. Elevation: flat landings around the spawn and each target (`LANDING` = 0.75 m) and around each bend (radius `half-width · tan(turn/2)` plus a cell, at most 30% of a straight). The rest of the spine, measured without the landings, takes alternating climbs and descents (`SEGMENT` ≈ 5 m each, at least two, a random first direction). A climb rises `U(0.5, 1) · min(hill_rise, length · tan(climb_grade))`, a descent falls the same with `descent_grade`. When `cliff_height` > 0, a descent holds a cliff with probability 1/2: a drop of `U(0.5, 0.9) · cliff_height` between two knots 1 µm apart, so across one hfield cell.
  3. Grid and corridor: 0.25 m nodes over the spine's bounding box plus 4 m (over 256 a side retries). Each node takes the height of its nearest spine point; nodes within half the corridor width of the spine are the corridor. Then value noise (`noise_amplitude`, uniform lattice values in ±amplitude every 2 m, smoothstep between). The kill plane is 2 m below the lowest corridor node, rounded to the millimetre, and every other node sits `PIT` = 1 m below it.
  4. Hazards: `floor(hazards_per_m · route_length + 0.5)` boxes (half-sizes 0.5, 0.5, 0.25), each at a random spine point and lateral offset within the corridor, at least 2 m from the spawn and every target, with no node of the flood fill's path within its footprint plus a cell. The box is centred 0.5 m above the ground, at shell-centre height, so the marker test catches a shell rolling through it. 20 tries per hazard, then the attempt fails.
  5. The flood fill again with the hazard footprints blocked, then `time_limit_s = path length / 5 m/s · time_slack`.
  - Markers: targets are 1 m spheres at the vertex, 0.5 m above the node below (shell-centre height). The spawn is at the first vertex, 0.5 m above the highest corridor node within 0.5 m, facing the first straight; `follow_yaw_deg` points from the spawn to the last target.
- Survive layout: `generate` draws `LAYOUTS[int(rng.random() · 6)]` once, before the attempts, so retries stay in the layout and each of plateau, ridge, obstacles, platform, seesaw and collapse gets 1/6 of the seeds at every difficulty. Plateau and ridge both carry drift and moving hazards, so those mechanics get 1/3; each body mechanic 1/6.
- Survive (`_survive`): the plateau layout is a disc: a flat top of `survive_disc_radius` at z = 0, the region a vertical capsule of that radius from 0 to 1 m, centred at shell-centre height (0.5 m), so the goal vector to the region's centre lies flat. Otherwise a ridge: a flat top `survive_ridge_width` wide along a random-yaw 8 m segment through the origin, the region a horizontal capsule along it at 0.5 m. Both fall off at `survive_falloff_grade` to a grid edge 4 m past the flat top; no noise. Spawn at the region's centre, (0, 0, 0.5); the time limit is `survive_duration`.
  - Movers (draws after the layout's, so a level with neither keeps its layout draws). When `survive_drift_speed` > 0 the region takes an `oscillate` motion with phase 0 (so it starts at the spawn): along the ridge with amplitude 2 m, or along a random horizontal axis on the plateau with amplitude half the disc radius (`DRIFT_REACH` = 0.5). Its centre so stays on the flat top. The period is `2π · amplitude / speed`, rounded to the millisecond, so the peak speed is the table's.
  - `moving_hazards` spheres of radius 0.4 m (`MOVER_RADIUS`) at shell-centre height, each oscillating 3 m (`MOVER_SWEEP`) either side of its centre along a random horizontal axis, at a random phase, period from `hazard_speed` as above. The first is centred on the spawn, so a hamsterball that stands still is hit; the rest on a random point of the flat top.
  - The dodge check (`_dodge`, below) runs whenever the region or a hazard moves.
- Obstacles (`_obstacles`): the plateau (no drift, no moving hazards) with `obstacles` solid pillars, vertical capsules (`PILLAR`: radius 0.4 m, half-length 0.5 m, foot on the ground) as driven bodies, each oscillating 3 m either side of its centre along a random horizontal axis at `obstacle_speed`, random phase. The first is centred on the spawn, the rest on a random point of the disc. The dodge check runs with the pillars as movers of radius 0.4 m: a pillar shoves rather than ends the episode, so keeping clear of it is stricter than surviving it.
- Platform and seesaw (`_slab`): no terrain (`geoms: []`), kill plane at −2 m, one 4 m square slab (`SLAB` = 2 m half-side, 0.2 m thick, top at z = 0) in `bodies`, and the region a box over it (`[2, 2, 0.5]` at 0.6 m in the slab's frame, `attach: 0`). The platform oscillates 3 m (`PLATFORM_SWEEP`) along a random axis at `platform_speed`, phase 0 so it starts under the spawn at the origin, and from d > 0 rocks `platform_tilt` about a random horizontal axis every 7 s (`TILT_PERIOD`). The seesaw is a ball joint (`SEESAW`: damping 20, range 15°, mass 20 kg) of `seesaw_stiffness`; the spawn is `seesaw_offset` from the pivot in a random direction, so the ball's weight tips it. No solvability check: the slab never leaves the ball nowhere to stand; the scripted-controller test is the check.
- Collapse (`_tiles`): no terrain, kill plane at −2 m, a grid of 7 or 9 by 7 or 9 box tiles (one draw per side) 1.5 m apart (`TILE_PITCH`, half-side 0.74 m, top at z = 0) centred on the spawn's tile, each falling `tile_delay` after the first touch, so at most 81 tiles (ticket 06: under about 100). The region is a box over the whole floor. The time limit is `min(survive_duration, 0.5 · tiles · delay)` (`TILE_USE`): a ball must leave each tile before it falls, so the tiles hold it for at most about tiles × delay, and at d = 1 (0.5 s, 60 s) the duration alone would ask for more than the floor has.
- Quantization (`_hfield`): node heights map to uint16 so the lowest is 0 and the highest 65535 (MuJoCo rescales samples to that range anyway); `pos` z is the lowest height and `size[2]` the span, each rounded to the millimetre. Every later step (flood fill, marker heights, kill plane for survive) reads the heights MuJoCo will see, `z0 + q / 65535 · span`. Row 0 is the +y edge (see [level.md](level.md)).
- Solvability (`_reach`, `_astar`): A* over 8-neighbour node steps, leg by leg from the spawn's node to each target's node in spine order (for unlock goal, the keys then the goal). A step passes if it climbs at most 15° (`dh ≤ tan 15° · step length`) and drops at most `tan 30° · step length + cliff_height`. Nodes outside the corridor (below the kill plane) and hazard footprints block. The path length is the sum of the legs' lengths.
- Survive dodge check (`_dodge`): a time-expanded flood fill over the flat-top nodes for the whole survive duration. Layer k is time `k · CELL / DODGE_SPEED` (`DODGE_SPEED` = 2.5 m/s, so one 4-neighbour cell per layer). The reachable set starts at the spawn's node, grows by one cell per layer (or stays), and keeps only the nodes safe at that layer's time: on the flat top, at least a cell plus one layer's drift inside the moving region, and more than mover radius + shell radius + half a cell + one layer of mover travel from every mover's centre at that time (moving hazards, or obstacle pillars). An empty set fails the attempt. Moving hazards count by their pose at each layer, not by their swept volume, so a level can make the hamsterball step aside as a hazard passes. The spawn's node must be safe at t = 0, so a spawn inside a hazard at t = 0 never ships (the loader does not check this).

## Invariants and checks

- Same (task, seed, difficulty, version) → same level id, on every platform: `uv run pytest tests/test_generator.py -k golden` over 5 tasks × d ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} × seeds 0–7 (200 levels, about 1 s). It prints the resample failure rate and fails above 25%.
- Any change to the output bumps `ProceduralGenerator.version` and the golden hashes.
- Every generated level loads through `Level.from_dict`, so it passes the loader's checks (256×256 grid, 0.25 m cells).
- `uv run pytest tests/test_generator.py`: provenance, the table rows and overrides, marker counts, cliffs only from d = 0.5, `GenerationError`, the time limit against the straight-line route, survive's six layouts at about 1/6 each over 120 seeds, plateau and ridge regions with the spawn at the region's centre, each body layout's table rows at d = 0 and 1, a collapse time limit within half of tiles × delay, every layout at d = 0 survived by a scripted controller (seeds 0–35: `toward_target` on all but collapse, a 2 m/s square spiral over the tile centres on collapse), hazard counts, the hamsterball resting at a spawn, the test_tasks scripted controller finishing reach goal, waypoint race, and unlock goal at d = 0.2, an inputless survive on a d = 0 plateau, survive levels still at d = 0, the drifting region's centre on the flat top, moving-hazard counts and speeds with the spawn clear at t = 0, and an inputless ball failing survive from d = 0.5 (seeds 0–11, which hold every layout but obstacles). The drift and hazard tests run on the first eight plateau or ridge levels by seed.

## Known quirks

- Platform-stable output rests on PCG64, IEEE arithmetic, `sqrt`, and the platform's libm for `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, and `atan`. Their results feed only values that are then rounded (millimetres, uint16), so a last-bit libm difference changes a level only if it lands exactly on a rounding boundary. The golden test on both CI platforms is the check.
- Overrides change the output, but the provenance block carries no overrides: two generators with different tables give the same (task, seed, difficulty, version) different levels. The canonical level, which trajectories embed, is still exact.
- Short legs bend less: at d = 0 with 3 waypoints over 5 m the turn limit is near 0, so easy routes are near straight, and reach goal at low d is one straight.
- The corridor can still fold back near an earlier straight. When neighbouring corridor nodes map to spine points more than 1 m apart and differ in height by more than a 15° cell step, the attempt retries (`_seams`). No sweep so far has hit it.
- Measured 2026-10-22 over the golden sweep (version 3): resample failure rate 18.4% (45 of 245 attempts). Route tasks retry 4 times, all at d = 1 from hazard placement in the 3 m corridor. Survive retries 41 times, 31 of them at d = 1, where a 1.5 m plateau holds three movers; the dodge check is the limit there. The survive sweep takes about 1 s (the dodge check, up to 480 layers at d = 1). Generation takes about 0.5 ms (d = 0) to 10 ms (d = 1, reach goal) per level.
- Measured 2026-10-22 over the golden sweep (version 4, macOS arm64 only): resample failure rate 5.2% (11 of 211 attempts); seeds 0–7 hold no obstacles level. Survive alone over seeds 0–47 × the five difficulties: 24.8% (79 of 319), every retry a dodge-check failure on plateau (46), obstacles (22) or ridge (13); platform, seesaw and collapse never retry. Generation per level: platform, seesaw and collapse 0.1–0.3 ms; plateau, ridge and obstacles 6 ms (d = 0 obstacles) to 88 ms (a d = 1 plateau, the layout that retries most); route tasks 3–19 ms.
- Measured 2026-10-22, scripted controllers on survive, seeds 0–39 per difficulty: `toward_target` survives every platform (12° tilt included) and every seesaw level at d = 0.5, 0.75 and 1, so neither body layout is hard for a tracker; an inputless ball falls off both from d = 0.5 (platform at 0.5: 0 of 7 survive). Obstacles: `toward_target` 6 of 6 at d = 0, 2 of 7 at d = 0.5, 2 of 7 at d = 0.75, 0 of 7 at d = 1; inputless 0 of 7 at each. A tracker steers back into a pillar's path and is shoved off, so obstacles want a controller that dodges. Collapse: the spiral survives 7 of 7 at d = 0 at 1.5 or 2 m/s and none at 3 m/s (it overshoots the corners onto fallen tiles).
- The dodge check grants the hamsterball 2.5 m/s from a standstill with no acceleration limit. The shell reaches 3 m/s in about a second on the flat, so a level can demand a sharper start than the ball has. No scripted controller tests a d = 1 survive level for survival; the tests check the region stays on the flat top, hazards start clear of the spawn, and an inputless ball fails from d = 0.5.
- The scripted controller of test_tasks.py (3 m/s cap, no planning) finishes 18 of 18 levels at d = 0.2, 13 of 18 at d = 0.5, and 0 of 18 at d = 0.8 (reach goal, waypoint race, collect items, seeds 0–5): the table's upper half needs a controller that climbs and plans.

## Decisions

- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10: `generator.py` lives in `core` beside `level.py`, since it builds a `Level` and the env imports both.
- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10: a cliff step passes the flood fill if it drops at most 30° of descent plus the cliff height, not the cliff height alone: a cliff sits on a descent, so the node pair across it carries the descent's slope too, and a cliff close to the cliff height would otherwise always fail.
- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10: hazards are centred at shell-centre height (0.5 m above the ground) rather than resting on the ground with their top at 0.5 m, where the marker test would catch a rolling shell only on the box's boundary.
- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10: "off the cells the flood fill uses" is read as off the path the first flood fill found (plus one cell), not off every reachable cell, which would leave no room for hazards in the corridor.
- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10: shape constants spec §8 leaves open are module constants, not table rows: 45° bends, 8 m straights, 5 m hill segments, 0.75 m landings, the 8 m ridge, a 1 m pit, 20 tries per hazard, 2 m hazard clearance.
- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10: `generate()` returning `(level, attempts)` is public so the golden test can report the failure rate without the generator keeping state.

- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/07: survive's moving hazards are spheres (any hazard can move since ticket 02), not the route tasks' boxes: a sphere's swept test has no rotation term, and its pose is its centre.
- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/07: the first moving hazard always sweeps through the spawn, so from d = 0.5 (two movers, a drifting region) standing still fails; on the plateau the drift alone (half the radius) never carries the region off a still ball.
- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/08: the layout is drawn once per `generate`, before the attempts. Redrawn per attempt (07's 50/50 did so), a layout that retries a lot loses its share: the d = 1 plateau fell to 0 of 48 seeds, replaced by layouts that never retry.
- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/08: `survive_disc_radius` at d = 1 is 2 m, not 1.5 m. With the layout fixed per seed, 15 of 31 d = 1 plateau seeds failed all 32 attempts (three movers and a drifting region on a 1.5 m disc); at 2 m none fail and the mean is 4.6 attempts. Under 07's per-attempt redraw those seeds had quietly become ridges.
- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/08: each body layout holds one mechanic (no drift or moving hazards on it), so a policy's success on a layout says which mechanic it handles. Obstacles stand on the plateau, the others over nothing, as the bundled `survive_raft`, `survive_seesaw` and `survive_collapse` do.
- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/08: obstacles are round pillars, not `survive_pushers`' bars, so the dodge check treats them as discs like the moving hazards.
- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/07: the dodge check tests hazards at each time, not by their swept volume. A swept-volume rule (no region point a hazard's path ever covers) can't hold once the first hazard is centred on the spawn, and leaves little of a 1.5 m plateau at d = 1.

## Tried and rejected

- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/08: a platform tilt of 0 → 8°. An inputless ball rode 3 of 7 platform levels for the whole 35 s at d = 0.5 (4°); 12° at d = 1 (6° at 0.5) drops every one, and `toward_target` still rides 12°.

- 2026-10-22, ticket survive-movers/07: four moving hazards of radius 0.5 at up to 2.5 m/s with a 2 m/s dodge speed. Two of eight d = 1 seeds failed all 32 attempts; three hazards of 0.4 m at up to 2 m/s with a 2.5 m/s dodge speed give an 18% resample rate over the sweep.

- 2026-10-22, ticket hamsterball-v1/10 (design, before code): heights from the nearest spine point with no landings. On the inner side of a bend, the nearest point jumps from one straight to the next, a height jump of `2 · half-width · tan(turn/2) · grade` (about 1.3 m for a 12 m corridor, a 45° bend, and a 15° climb), which would put walls and cliffs into levels below d = 0.5. Flat landings at bends remove the jump.
